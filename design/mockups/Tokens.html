Humanloop visual language

One question organises the whole interface: whose turn is it? Mint always means you, blue always means Claude, and everything else stays grey. Dark, quiet and dense: the terminal is the only thing that should ever be loud.

State language

The same shape at two sizes: a glyph on each tab and a dot per tab in the sidebar. Filled means attention, outlined means settled. The queue a task sits in says whose turn it is, so rows carry no colour of their own.

Rules the design keeps

—Mint appears only where something is waiting on you: ages, tab glyphs and the Next button.
—Blue is never urgent. A blue task is safe to ignore, so it never flashes or counts in the Dock badge.
—Shortcuts are taught quietly: a chip on the Next button, the rest in tooltips, menus and the shortcuts screen. Nothing competes with the terminal.
—Opening a task never moves it. Only Claude starting or stopping, or Park and Done, change a queue.
